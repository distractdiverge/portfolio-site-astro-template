# Direction

**Status:** The visual blend is confirmed by the owner (unchanged). The content direction was added 2026-11-12 from [[case-study-2026-11]]. Rules 1, 5 and 6 rest on owner decisions; the rest are proposed. Tokens are in `tokens/`.

## Visual blend (confirmed, unchanged)

- **Utility, 70%:** functional, honest, no-nonsense, "crisp paper." Matches "clean, no-nonsense, focused on perfection without clutter."
- **Warmth, 30%:** passionate, collaborative, inclusive. Keeps the site from feeling cold.

How it shows up in tokens:

- Warm paper tint with a system font stack and tight, honest hierarchy.
- Small radii and one soft shadow, with purple accents and personal imagery.
- Subtle motion only, with the rainbow fern and small icon quirks.

The case study supports this. Light static builds with plain typography (jvns.ca, tatianamac.com) earned trust. Heavy builders with sprawling nav (Namaste Data on Wix) did not.

## Content direction (proposed)

The pattern that works best for technical solo consultants is **writing as proof, plus one clear service page.** Hillel Wayne, Nicolas Carlo and Jonathan Stark all lead with their writing, and each still has a page that says: here is what I'm paid to do, here is the evidence, here is how to reach me.

That gives five rules for the content:

1. **Offer first, story second.** The first screen says what Astrid fixes and for whom, in literal words. The personal story, photos and values come right after, not before.
2. **jvns.ca for readability, not for the business model.** Julia Evans' site has no consulting path. Copy its plain titles, generous tone and "here's what I learned" voice, but add the paid-work layer it lacks.
3. **Values shown as policies.** Sliding scale, capacity limits, open-source defaults and how Astrid uses AI are written as concrete practices, not slogans. See [[voice]].
4. **Transparency as the differentiator.** Every credible site in the sample has clarity, writing and a human contact path. What separates sites is published prices, honest capacity and an equity policy. These are cheap for a company of one and match Astrid's values.
5. **Anti-capitalist in practice, subtle in wording.** A confirmed core value (2026-11-12). No ads and no trackers. Values carried by pricing, ownership and money-transparency mechanisms, with a few signals people in the know will recognize. See [[voice]].
6. **Narrow the offers.** Two offers, one people track and one on-ramp, instead of five equal service lines. A referrer has to be able to summarize Astrid in one sentence. See [[content-strategy]].

## How the two halves fit

| Visual (confirmed) | Content (proposed) |
|---|---|
| Crisp sheet of paper, small digestible sections | Each section answers one buyer question |
| Rainbow fern, purple accents, personal photos | Values and identity carried by imagery and policies, not by explanation |
| No dense text | Short service pages with listed deliverables and a price |
| Subtle motion only | No pop-ups, countdowns or attention tricks |

- **Precision (sharp and dense):** rejected by the owner because it means dense text. Still rejected.
