# Voice and copy

**Status:** New 2026-11-12 from [[case-study-2026-11]]. The "Hidden in plain sight" section reflects the owner's decision on 2026-11-12 (see [[003-owner-decisions-2026-11-12]]). The rest is proposed. The vocabulary list carries over from [[principles]], which the owner confirmed.

## The voice in one line

Write like an engineering design doc with warmth: plain, specific and honest about trade-offs, and clearly written by a person.

## Rules

- **Literal before clever.** The first sentence of every page says what the page is about. NN/g found that users "see right through" jargon and corporate speak.
- **Use the reader's own pain words.** "Nobody fully understands this code" beats "legacy modernization." Nicolas Carlo's "valuable code you're afraid to change" is the model.
- **Name a number when you have one.** Price, timeline, response time, capacity.
- **Say what you don't do.** A "who this is not for" line makes the rest more believable.
- **First person, singular.** Rainbowfern is a company of one. Don't use "we" or "our team."
- **Short chunks.** One idea per paragraph, a heading every few paragraphs. No walls of text (rule from [[principles]]).
- **People with unstated pronouns get they/them** in examples and copy.

## Vocabulary (carried over, confirmed)

postmortem, reliability, handoff, tradeoff, scope, accessibility, fair/balanced, inquisitive, passionate, collaborative

**Proposed additions:** map, document, safe to change, plain language, self-owned, sliding scale, capacity, systems (always with a concrete noun attached).

## Words to avoid

unlock, leverage, transform, empower, cutting-edge, seamless, synergy, game-changer, supercharge, revolutionize, world-class, "in today's fast-paced world," "AI-powered" as a selling point.

Also avoid growth-at-all-costs language: hustle, scale (as a goal in itself), disrupt, crush it, 10x, maximize ROI, human capital.

Also avoid:

- Tidy triads of abstractions ("innovation, excellence, and impact").
- Testimonials about how life-changing Astrid is.
- Emoji in headings and buttons, because screen readers announce them.
- False urgency ("only 2 spots left!") unless it's a real, dated capacity statement.

## Values as policies, not slogans

The values-forward sites that still get hired state their values as operating practices. Draft lines for "How I work" (to edit):

- "I'm neurodivergent and queer, and I build that into how I work: written agendas before calls, async-friendly communication, no surprise video."
- "I use AI tools as collaborators, and I tell you where and how."
- "I default to open-source, self-hostable, data-sovereign tools, and I explain the trade-offs when I don't."
- "Sliding-scale pricing for co-ops, nonprofits and social-justice organizations. Same quality at every rate. I don't ask about income or identity."

Pattern: be explicit about **who you prioritize**, not about who you exclude. Mandy Brown, Meena Das and Tatiana Mac each name systems of oppression directly and still sell to companies.

## Hidden in plain sight: anti-capitalism as a core value

**Decided 2026-11-12:** Rainbowfern runs as anti-capitalist and pro-socialist as possible. On the site, that shows up subtly. A general client reads it as fair, people-first business. People who know the tradition recognize the signals.

**Rules:**

- **No ads.** No third-party trackers. Basic, privacy-respecting site analytics only.
- **Don't use the labels** ("anti-capitalist," "socialist") in headings, offer copy or the hero.
- **Show it through mechanisms,** which are also the strongest trust signals in the case study: sliding scale, solidarity rate, published fee allocation, honest capacity, co-ops first, self-owned tools.

**Signals people will recognize** (pick a few and don't stack them all on one page):

- **Pricing line:** "Pay what your situation allows. You'll get the same work either way." It echoes "from each according to ability, to each according to need" without quoting it.
- **Solidarity rate** as the name of the lowest tier. **Mutual aid** for free help (the mentoring page's free list).
- **Ownership language:** "Tools should belong to the people who use them." "You own everything I build: no lock-in, no rent." It points to the means of production without naming them.
- **Who comes first:** "Co-ops, worker-owned businesses, nonprofits and community groups come first."
- **Where the money goes:** a fee-allocation note on `/pricing` (pay, health care, the solidarity pool, tools, learning). It quietly says "not shareholders."
- **"Bread and roses"** as a small easter egg: a footer line, a `/now` sign-off, or a tiny rose among the site's icon quirks. People who know labor history will catch it; everyone else sees a nice phrase.
- **Colophon:** built with open-source tools, hosted independently, no ads, no trackers.

**Avoid:** party symbols and slogans, and anything that makes a small-business client feel judged for running a business. The critique is aimed at extraction, not at the people Astrid serves.

## Proof copy

- Name employers and specifics: PayPal, PayPal Credit, Swift Financial, Razorfish, Drexel.
- Testimonials, when Astrid collects them: full name and title, and about a concrete change, not about Astrid's personality (Lara Hogan's pattern).
- Mark what Astrid personally did versus what a team did.
