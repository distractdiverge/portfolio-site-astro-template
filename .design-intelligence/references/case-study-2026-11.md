# Case study: 18 independent consultant sites (2026-11)

**Source:** The research report "What Makes Independent Consultant Sites Drive Paid Inquiries," run 2026-11-12 in the Independent Contracting project. This is a condensed copy so design sessions can use it without the full report.

## Question

Which content on solo-consultant sites gets visitors to make contact for paid work?

## Answer in one paragraph

The pages that work answer four questions fast and in plain words: what you fix, for whom, why you can be trusted, and what the next step costs. Every credible site has clarity, writing and a human contact path. What separates sites is transparency: published prices, honest capacity and an equity policy.

## Patterns, with frequency (lower bounds)

- Clear statement of what they do in the first screen: about 15 of 18
- Direct human contact path: about 15 of 18
- Writing, newsletter or RSS as proof: about 14 of 18
- Named employers or clients: about 11 of 18
- Talks or speaking page: about 9 of 18
- A book, course or zine: 8 of 18
- Equity, sliding-scale or free-help mechanism: 7 of 18
- Testimonials with names: about 6 of 18
- Explicit availability or capacity status: 4 of 18
- Consulting prices published in dollars: 3 of 18
- Free intro call through a scheduler: 2 to 3 of 18
- Audience-segmented entry ("choose your path"): 2 of 18

## Anti-patterns seen

- Abstract heroes that delay saying what the person does (TechBetter).
- A one-sentence homepage. It works for a well-known speaker and fails for a new practice (Tatiana Mac).
- Navigation sprawl and heavy builders (Namaste Data: about 15 nav items, Wix, tag managers).
- Stale consulting pages for work the person no longer takes.
- Emoji in headings. Hard-sell sales-letter copy.

## Caveats

- Some sites changed by 2026-11-12: Mentrix is now a placeholder, and Adrian Roselli is open to consulting again. See [[example-sites-observations]].

- The buyer statistics (Hinge) cover professional-services buyers broadly. The NN/g findings cover organizational About pages. Both transfer to a company of one, but not perfectly.
- No fractional-CTO site was verified. Some sites were assessed from partial pages.
- The `llms.txt` studies are vendor-run.
- Stark and Morgan sell advice, and their claims come from experience, not controlled research.
